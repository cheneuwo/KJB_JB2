In the 7th and 6th centuries B.C., the tribes of the Arabian desert poured northward and overran the land of [Edom](wiki:Edom). Obadiah described the fall of the Edomites with unrestrained mockery and delight, seeing in their downfall a sign of God's punishment for those who attacked Israel. He particularly relished the fact that the citadels of [Edom](wiki:Edom), build atop mountains as high as any in Israel, no longer protected them. The capital of the edomites was [Sela](Sela_(Edom)) (Hebrew for "rock"), which [King Amaziah](wiki:Amaziah_of_Judah) of Judah once took by storm ({ref}`2 Kgs.<KINGS_2>` {ref}`14<KINGS_2_14>`:{ref}`7<KINGS_2_14_7>`). [Sela](Sela_(Edom)) was later called [Petra](wiki:Petra) (Latin for "rock") when it was the capital of the [Nabateans](wiki:Nabataeans). This people, who had come out of the Arabian desert in the 4th century B.C., carved elaborate temples and tombs, from the variegated sandstone that forms the cliffs of [Petra](wiki:Petra).
