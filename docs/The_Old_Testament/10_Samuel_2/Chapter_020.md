To quell an uprising led by [Sheba](Sheba_son_of_Bichri) the Benjamite against {ref}`David<WHO_David>`, {ref}`Joab<WHO_Joab>`, the commander of Israel's forces, put the walled city of [Abel-beth-maachah under siege](wiki:Abel-beth-maachah). The siege was terminated when [Sheba](Sheba_son_of_Bichri) was decapitated by the inhabitants of the city and his head given to {ref}`Joab<WHO_Joab>`. The Assyrian relief reproduced heree was discovered at [Calah](wiki:Nimrud) and is from the reign of [Asshurnasirpal II](wiki:Ashurnasirpal_II) (first half of the 9th century B.C.). It illustrates in fine detail various methods of assult used in ascient times. In the scene a battering ram, with an axed-shaped blade attached to its end, is being propelled forward and backward against the stones and bricks of the wall, while archers in a movable siege tower harass the defenders on the parapet. The besieged troops are trying to deflect the battering ram with chains; to frustrate this stratagem, two Assyrian soldiers are striving to pull the chains away with hooks. At the left a storming party attempts to scale the wall on a ladder, while two soldiers, protected by heavy coats of mail, are engaged in breaking down the fortifications with pikes and spears. At bottom center, other Assyrians are trying to undermine the city wall.
