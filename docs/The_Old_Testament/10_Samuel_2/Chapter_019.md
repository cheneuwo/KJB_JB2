The prohibition against making images was a basic tenet of the children of Israel. Unlike other deities of the time, the God of Israel was far beyond human imagination. No image or symbol ivented by men could represent him "Who is like unto thee, O LORD, among the gods?" (Ex. 15:11). A famous image of the 18th century B.C. is a diorite stele found at Susa, capital of the asient Elam. It contains the Code of Hammurabi (laws governing Babylonian public and private life) and the figure of King Hammurabi standing before Shamash, the god of righteousness, to receive the symbols of kingship and justice.
