The Assyrian empire was ruled from 745 to 727 B.C. by dynamic [King Tiglath-pileser III](wiki:Tiglath-Pileser_III), who was also known as {ref}`Pul<WHO_Pul>`. [Tiglath-pileser](wiki:Tiglath-Pileser_III) attacked [Syria](wiki:Syria_(region)) and [Palestine](wiki:Palestine_(region)) a number of times, deporting his captives to distant regions and turning their lands into vassal states. In an effort to save his country from such a fate, [King Menahem](wiki:Menahem), who ruled over Israel in [Samaria](wiki:Samaria), paid the Assyrian monarch silver, which he extracted from "the mighty men of wealth, of each man fifty shekels of silver" ({ref}`2 Kgs.<KINGS_2>` {ref}`15<KINGS_2_15>`:{ref}`20<KINGS_2_15_20>`). A fragment of a relief found at [Calah](wiki:Nimrud) shows the likeness of [King Tiglath-pileser III](wiki:Tiglath-Pileser_III). This Gypsum wall panel relif depicting Tiglath-Pileser III is housed by [The British Museum](https://www.britishmuseum.org/), [Assert Number 118900](https://www.britishmuseum.org/collection/object/W_1856-0909-61).
