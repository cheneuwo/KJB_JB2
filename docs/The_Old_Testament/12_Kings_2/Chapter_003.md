The kingdom of Moab was long dominated by Israel and forced to pay tribute. But when Jehoram (Joram) came to the throne of Israel, succeeding his brother Ahaziah, the Moabites revolted. These and subsequent events are recorded on the [Moabite Stone](wiki:Mesha_Stele), erected by Mesha, the Moabite king who lead the revolt. The stone, shown here, is a slab of black basalt, about fourty inches high and twenty-seven inches wide. The inscription on it is written in Moabite, a tongue similar to Biblical Hebrew, and recounts Mesha's brave deeds and the story of Moab's enslavement and subsequent liberation from Israel. The stone was discovered in 1868 at Dibon in Transjordan.
