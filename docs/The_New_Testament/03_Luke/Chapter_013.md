Siloam was a section of Jerusalem around the [Pool of Siloam](wiki:Pool_of_Siloam) (see map here), mentioned in {ref}`John<JOHN>` {ref}`9<JOHN_9>`:{ref}`7<JOHN_9_7>`,{ref}`11<JOHN_9_11>`. The exact location of the tower to which {ref}`Jesus<WHO_Jesus>` referred is not known. The original name of the small reservoir was the Pool of Shiloah. In {ref}`Nehemiah<NEHEMIAH>` {ref}`3<NEHEMIAH_3>`:{ref}`15<NEHEMIAH_3_15>` it is called "the pool of Siloah by the King's garden". The Hebrew word "shiloah" means a "sending" of water, that is, a conduit. At the time of [King Ahaz](wiki:Ahaz) (735-715 B.C.) "the conduit of the upper pool" ({ref}`Is.<ISAIAH>` {ref}`7<ISAIAH_7>`:{ref}`3<ISAIAH_7_3>`) brought the water from the [Spring of Gihon](wiki:Gihon_Spring) (see map {ref}`here<fig:map_Jerusalem_Nehemiah>`) around the eastern side of Jerusalem into a pool at the south end of the city. {ref}`Isaiah<WHO_Isaiah>` speaks of the water in this conduit as "the waters of shiloah that go softly"  ({ref}`Is.<ISAIAH>` {ref}`8<ISAIAH_8>`:{ref}`6<ISAIAH_8_6>`). [King Hezekiah](wiki:Hezekiah) (715-687 B.C.), the son of [Ahaz](wiki:Ahaz), in anticipation of an [Assyrian](wiki:Assyria) siege of the city, had the water diverted from this exposed conduit into a tunnel that his workmen cut through the rock (see {ref}`here<fig:Hezekiah's_Tunnel>`) in order to bring the water into the [Pool of Shiloah](wiki:Pool_of_Siloam) inside the walls of the city. The modern [Pool of Siloam](wiki:Pool_of_Siloam), seen here, is at the site of the ancient pool, although the water around it are of recent construction. The water flows today into the pool from [Hezekiah](wiki:Hezekiah)'s tunnel as it did in the time of {ref}`Jesus<WHO_Jesus>`.
