# Cain

(Kayn) HEBREW: QAYIN

*"smith"*

(The name *Cain* first appeared in  {ref}`Genesis<GENESIS>` {ref}`4<GENESIS_4>`:{ref}`1<GENESIS_4_1>`)

The firstborn son of **{ref}`Adam<WHO_Adam>`** and **{ref}`Eve<WHO_Eve>`**, Cain was a farmer and the older brother of the shepherd **{ref}`Abel<WHO_Abel>`**. When Cain offered in sacrifice the produce of the field (stintingly given, according to tradition, though the Bible does not say that), God disdained the offering, preferring {ref}`Abel<WHO_Abel>`'s sacrifice of the firstborn sheep of his flock. This enraged Cain, who summoned his brother out to a field and {ref}`killed him<GENESIS_4_8>`. Legend supplies another detail omitted from the bare biblical narrative: The murder weapon was a stone.

Calling Cain to account for the first murder in human history, the Lord asked him, "{ref}`Where is Abel your brother?<GENESIS_4_9>`" Refusing to accept responsibility for the deed, Cain claimed not know. "{ref}`Am I my brother's keeper?<GENESIS_4_9>`" ({ref}`Gen<GENESIS>`. {ref}`4<GENESIS_4>`:{ref}`9<GENESIS_4_9>`) he replied, with words that are among the most familiar of biblical questions. But as it turned out, that was not satisfactory answer. "{ref}`The voice of your brother's blood is crying to me from the ground<GENESIS_4_10>` ({ref}`Gen<GENESIS>`. {ref}`4<GENESIS_4>`:{ref}`10<GENESIS_4_10>`), the Lord told him.

Because of his crime, Cain was sentenced to wander the earth and, if he did settle anywhere to farm, his labors would be futile; the ground into which {ref}`Abel<WHO_Abel>`'s blood had flowed would refuse to produce crops for him. Fearing that he himself would be killed by anyone who encountered him, Cain begged for mercy. "{ref}`My punishment is greater than I can bear<GENESIS_4_13>`" ({ref}`Gen<GENESIS>`. {ref}`4<GENESIS_4>`:{ref}`13<GENESIS_4_13>`), he pleaded. God showed his forgiveness and mercy by promising to protect him with a mark (unexplained in {ref}`Genesis<GENESIS>` but perhaps a skin blemish or a tattoo) that would ward off attackers. It was a sign that the Lord himself would demand vengeance if Cain were killed. Then Cain started his wandering, "{ref}`in the land of Nod, east of Eden<GENESIS_4_16>`" ({ref}`Gen<GENESIS>`. {ref}`4<GENESIS_4>`:{ref}`16<GENESIS_4_16>`) -- the very word Nod meaning "wondering." According to tradition, {ref}`he build a city<GENESIS_4_17>` named for his son {ref}`Enoch<WHO_Enoch>`; his descendants are said to have included the earliest tent-dwelling herdsmen, metalworkers, and musicians.

Cain is mentioned twice in the New Testament. Christians are warned "{ref}`not to be like Cain<JOHN_1_3_12>` ({ref}`1 Jn<JOHN_1>`. {ref}`3<JOHN_1_3>`:{ref}`12<JOHN_1_3_12>`); ungodly people are said to "{ref}`walk in the way of Cain<JUDE_11>` ({ref}`Jude 11<JUDE_11>`).
